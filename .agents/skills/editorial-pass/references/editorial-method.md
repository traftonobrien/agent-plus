# Editorial Method

Use this reference for a detailed detect or edit pass. The governing rule is preservation before
style.

## Review order

1. Identify the draft's job, audience, medium, and author signals.
2. Mark protected evidence and exact language.
3. Locate pattern clusters, not isolated words or punctuation.
4. Make the minimum change that removes the reader problem.
5. Re-read for factual drift, meaning loss, flatness, and replacement formulas.

## High-value pattern groups

- **Unsupported importance:** A routine fact becomes a turning point, testament, transformation, or
  proof of a broad trend without evidence.
- **Empty setup:** The draft announces a point, insight, honesty, tension, or surprise before stating
  the useful sentence.
- **Formulaic structure:** Repeated binary contrasts, padded groups of three, dramatic fragments,
  identical paragraph shapes, or a conclusion that repeats the preceding text.
- **Vague authority:** Experts, reports, users, or research appear without an exact source.
- **Generic benefit:** Efficiency, alignment, confidence, momentum, or quality appears without a
  mechanism, owner, changed step, metric, or attributed claim.
- **Ornamental presentation:** Decorative bold, emoji, headings, colons, or punctuation organize
  appearance instead of meaning.

## Preserve when justified

Keep a passive construction when the actor is unknown, intentionally omitted, or not relevant.
Keep repeated technical terms when synonym changes would reduce precision. Keep deliberate rhythm,
fragments, contrasts, lists, and punctuation when the author sample supports them.

One pattern is not proof of machine authorship. A cluster is a reason to inspect the passage, not a
verdict about its origin.

## Evidence boundary

Never replace a vague claim with invented specificity. Ask for the missing mechanism, owner,
example, metric, or source. Preserve qualifications that limit scope, causality, risk, legal effect,
or technical meaning.

Do not add service promises, security claims, quality labels, future actions, or outcome claims that
the source does not support.
